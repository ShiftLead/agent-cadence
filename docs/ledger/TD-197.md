**Why:** every other TD-173 command, and the gate (TD-201), compare with or write the canonical form of an entry. ledgerboard v2.2 defines it (ledgerboard TD-005: `canonical()`, `entry_text()`, `edit_entry()`), so both consumers share one copy of the format and `ledger.py` only adds the side effects. `fmt` also gives the person a way to keep editing by hand: edit, run `fmt`, push.

**Fix:**
- `vendor_ledgerboard.sh v2.2`, with `MANIFEST` regenerated; the vendored fixtures bring v2.2's `ledger_edit` cases into `tests/ledgerboard/run.py`.
- `ledger.py fmt [TD-n …] [--check]`, defaulting to every entry in the ledger and archive, writes each through `canonical()`. An entry `canonical()` refuses is named with its hand fix and left alone. `--check` prints the entries that would change and exits 1, writing nothing.
- The first `fmt` over this ledger lands as its own PR, saying that every entry's file changes once, so no other change hides in it.
- cadence.md §2.8 points at SPEC's archived entry, and §2.12 states the canonical field order (SPEC's), with one `cadence-changes.md` entry for both.
- Tests: every entry in this repo's ledger and archive round-trips through `canonical()` or is named as refused (none today, counted 2026-10-29); `fmt` is idempotent; `fmt --check` writes nothing.

**Done when** v2.2 is vendored, `ledger.py fmt` puts a hand-edited entry in canonical form, and §2.8 and §2.12 say where the form is defined.
