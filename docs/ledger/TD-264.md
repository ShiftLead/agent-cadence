## TD-264: Board items and messages to the person are written for someone outside the work, checked by board_edit.py add

**Priority:** high
**Type:** feature
**Added:** 2026-11-14
**Status:** Open
**Id take:** eae6bfe4f8b146a1b205fe5c044aad58
**Blocked by:** TD-263

**Why:** Paul often can't follow what sessions write to him. He moves between projects all day, and each session is deep in one problem, so its board items and messages read as shorthand: file names, function names, plan numbers and terms the session coined. His words (2026-11-14): "How do we get our TD descriptions to be more human friendly? I believe I have raised this in other TDs before." He has: ShiftLead TD-1801 ("text formatted for humans") and TD-1816. So this should be one rule every writer follows, checked by a tool, not another one-off.

Two cases from 2026-11-14:
- A grinder's steer on ledgerboard TD-108 reached his Queue as one dense paragraph ("fixtures/grammar/'s item-line start… judged by item_problem()… text 13…"). In his words, it was "probably great for someone who is already tracking what grammar and a line start are - but to me they are gibberish until i know what grammar, what line is being talked about." The techlead's one-line recommendation under it was the clearest part.
- ShiftLead TD-1648's decide was well laid out, but its answers didn't say who acts. Does "Pinned, run it again" mean Paul edits his profile file himself, or a session does it on his answer?

cadence.md §3.3 already says to write for someone outside the work (TD-084, *An item explains itself, in plain English*). It is prose that no tool checks, and these items passed it. `board_edit.py add` is the one writer every repo's sessions use for a board item (TD-206), so a check there reaches all of them.

Asked for by a session of Paul's in ShiftLead/ledgerboard (ao-led-tds-ledger, three notes and an `ask`, 2026-11-14: m-6d7f329d3a51, m-8b05b8ab9151, m-522ca5fb0d17, m-5af1d1c51e0d). ShiftLead is asked separately for the Queue's side.

**Location:** files/scripts/board_edit.py (add: passthrough() ~223); files/docs/cadence.md §3.3 (TD-084's *An item explains itself, in plain English*; TD-122's *leads with one sentence*); files/docs/cadence-changes.md; tests/

**Fix:** Paul's rules, as his notes give them (2026-11-14), each checked mechanically, never by a model's judgment:

1. **The Question is one plain sentence:** at most 25 words, ending in `?`, with no code in it: no backticks, no paths, no `name()`, no file names.
2. **Every answer says who acts:** "I'll …" or "you …" for the person, or "a session …". An answer like "Pinned, run it again" is refused.
3. **Orientation comes first.** The Context's first sentence names the repo and the larger piece of work it belongs to (the umbrella entry's title in plain words), before any detail. A term the session coined is explained or left out.
4. **No length cap on Context** (Paul's correction, 2026-11-14: "longer context … is fine and likely necessary sometimes, we just need it to be geared towards a human reader"). It may be as long as it needs. It uses plain words and explains rather than cites. Ids, files and function names go behind the Open link or the synopsis, not in the lead.
5. **A session-to-session message shown on the Queue** leads with a plain one-line summary for the person (the recipient's recommendation, where there is one), with the original folded underneath. That is ShiftLead's to show; this repo writes the rule.

agent-cadence's part:
- Write the rule in `files/docs/cadence.md` §3.3, turning TD-084's paragraph's prose (*An item explains itself, in plain English*) into the checked rules above, and point the asks and steers to the person (§3.3's *leads with one sentence*) at the same rule.
- Check it in `board_edit.py add` before it hands the item to ledgerboard's `board.py add`. It refuses an item that breaks rules 1–3 and says which rule, in plain words. Where a check belongs in ledgerboard so ShiftLead's writer shares it, ask ledgerboard with `ao td add --repo ledgerboard` (cadence.md §3.7) and keep the agent-cadence check until it is vendored.
- Add a `files/docs/cadence-changes.md` entry and tests, one passing and one refused case per rule.

6. **Nothing that waits on the person is invisible** (Paul, 2026-11-14, on TD-261: "I do not see an item for 261"). TD-261 had an `**Ask:**` and `Owner: paul` but no `decision (paul)` gate and no board item, so no Queue showed it. The ledger writer (`ledger.py new` and `set`, and the cadence check's `ledger` row on a PR) refuses an open entry that has an `**Ask:**`, or an Owner who is the person, but no `decision (<person>)` gate. Once the gate is there the item follows: the cadence check's `gates` row wants a board item for each gate a PR adds, and the gate sweep files a `decide` for a decision no item covers (cadence.md §2.14). On 2026-11-14, six other open entries had the same gap: TD-156, TD-187, TD-194, TD-243, TD-247 and TD-249. Each had an Owner who is the person, or an `**Ask:**`, and no gate. The build gates each one, or says why it should not wait on the person. This check is ledgerboard's to own, since `ledger.py` is vendored: ask for it with `ao td add --repo ledgerboard`, and carry it in `check_cadence.py`'s `ledger` row until it is vendored. ShiftLead's m-1e898050c9f5 is the same gap on the board side, a board line whose Context is none.

Build it after TD-263: v3.0 changes the board to a directory and `board_edit.py add` with it.

**Done when:** `board_edit.py add` refuses a board item whose Question is longer than 25 words, doesn't end in `?`, or holds code; an answer that names no actor ("I'll", "you", "a session"); or a Context whose first sentence names no repo and larger piece of work. Each refusal says which rule in plain words. A test holds a passing and a refused case for each. cadence.md §3.3 states the rule, and cadence-changes.md records it. An open entry with an `**Ask:**`, or an Owner who is the person, but no `decision (<person>)` gate fails the ledger check (a test holds it).
