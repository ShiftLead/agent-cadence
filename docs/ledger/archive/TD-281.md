## TD-281: How a design's review rounds end is judged card by card: write the guidance down, and let each repo say which designs are far-reaching

**Priority:** High
**Type:** feature
**Added:** 2026-11-16 (Paul, through his session ao-led-tds-ledger; drafted by ao-agent-cadence-ac-techlead)
**Owner:** person
**Status:** Resolved 2026-11-18
**Id take:** d56a567618434ac5931a3a6056b45e29

**Why:** cadence.md §4.8 ends a design's review rounds by its stop rule or its cap, and its end-of-rounds item already carries the evidence: the trend of build-changing findings per round, the findings no review has read, and any recurring topic. What it does not say is how to choose among `Go`, `One more round` and `Hold` from that evidence; the item carries the author's recommendation, and the author is the designer. So the recommendation is judged case by case, by the seat least independent of the design, and the person has to re-judge it card by card.

During ledgerboard's v3 migration (2026-11-04 to 2026-11-16), an interactive session (`led-tds`) judged exactly those things on about 175 end-of-rounds cards. It disagreed with the card's own recommendation 41 times (23%), almost all on how a design's rounds should end, and Paul sided with it 31 times. That judgement can be written down and applied the same way in every repo.

It also says where the person's word is actually needed. Paul's layer (§2.4 add) holds every design's builds for his Go. Most designs are routine, and the ones that need him are the far-reaching ones: those whose builds change roles, briefs, the spec or the glossary that every session reads. Today no repo can say which those are, so the hold is all or nothing.

Paul approved filing this on 2026-11-16 (his word *file*, in session `led-tds`). Its siblings are filed separately: ledgerboard TD-132, and two ShiftLead entries, one of them making ShiftLead's techlead the judge.
