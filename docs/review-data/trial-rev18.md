# Trial of the rounds rules (plan revision 18) against design-rounds.json

Generated by `trial_rules.py` (stdlib, rerunnable). Rules: round = one pass through the design's own model list; stop after a round with no build-changing finding (cap round included); cap 4 in-repo / 6 wide; a cap that still raised ends the rounds and a board item goes to the person. Recurring topics are **not simulated**: topics are not recorded in the data and are not guessed, so the item rate below is the **at-cap rate only** (a lower bound on the real rate once the layer rule and recurring topics exist).

Skipped: 9 no per-review detail, 2 parallel. Designs simulated: 86 (in-repo 78, wide 8).

## The proxy, and its weakness (read this first)

Only the ledger-writer design has real `build_changing` counts (used as is: >= 1 raises, under both variants). Everywhere else a review "raises a build-changing finding" by proxy:

- **Primary:** verdict is not READY (NOT READY, REVISE, ...), or total_findings >= 3. READY with <= 2 findings raises none. A review with no verdict, only a count, raises when the count is >= 3. A review with neither verdict nor count (90 of 667 reviews) raises nothing.
- **Strict:** primary, plus any not-READY review with >= 1 finding; a review with neither verdict nor count raises (upper bound).

Weaknesses: total findings are not build-changing findings. Late reviews in these loops are mostly minor follow-ons (wording, a missed consistency edit, a miscount) that the proxy counts, and a not-READY verdict is given for any open finding however small. Both push the proxy toward **raising too often**: it overstates how long a loop runs, understates stops, and overstates cap items. Under the primary proxy the verdict rule dominates (the 3-finding rule only adds verdictless counts); where a review has a verdict null and a count of 1-2 the primary proxy lets it stop, which is the other, smaller, error direction. The ledger-writer design shows the gap: its reviews 7-10 carry 1-1.5 build-changing findings out of far more total findings, and review 11 reports none. Treat the cap-item rates as an upper-leaning estimate and the saved-review counts as a lower-leaning estimate.
